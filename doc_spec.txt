1. Executive Summary

The application already covers the core motorcycle ownership workflow: initial setup, garage dashboard, fuel tracking, maintenance schedules, pre-ride inspection, vehicle documents, and backup/restore. The recommended direction is to evolve it into a reliable Motorcycle Ownership & Maintenance Assistant rather than adding many advanced features before the existing workflows are dependable.

Top priorities

Make fuel-economy calculations correct for full-tank, partial-fill, edited, deleted, and out-of-order records.

Make maintenance due calculations work independently by distance and calendar date.

Use motorcycle-specific manufacturer guidance for tyre pressure, chain slack, and maintenance intervals.

Protect data during backup import, reset, migration, and storage failures.

Add reminders, expense analytics, fuel economy trends, and a repair journal after the fundamentals are reliable.

3. Critical Logic and Bug-Prevention Requirements

3.1 Fuel and mileage calculation

For a full-tank-to-full-tank interval, calculate distance as the current valid full-tank odometer minus the previous valid full-tank odometer. Divide that distance by the fuel consumed during the interval. This assumes the tank was filled to a comparable full level at both endpoints.

Required safeguards:

Do not treat the first refuel as a completed mileage measurement.

Do not calculate an interval mileage from a partial refill as if it were a full refill.

When partial refills occur, accumulate fuel until the next valid full-tank endpoint, or clearly use a different documented method.

Show mileage as Pending/Not enough data until a valid interval exists.

Warn if a new odometer reading is below the previous reading; allow correction only with an explicit confirmation or correction workflow.

When an old entry is edited or deleted, recompute every affected subsequent interval, lifetime average, cost per kilometre, and chart.

For lifetime mileage, calculate total valid distance divided by total fuel attributed to valid intervals; do not take a simple average of trip mileage values.

Validate litres and price as positive values; handle zero, missing, and implausible entries without crashing.

3.2 Maintenance and service due logic

Distance-based and date-based schedules must be evaluated independently. A maintenance item is due if either configured trigger is reached. The UI should distinguish Upcoming, Due Soon, Overdue, and Not Configured.

Support both kilometre and calendar intervals where appropriate.

Do not hardcode one universal interval for every motorcycle or component.

Use manufacturer recommendations or user-configured values for oil, filters, coolant, brake fluid, tyres, chain slack and other items.

Keep a record of what changed, when, at what odometer, by whom (optional), and at what cost.

Editing an interval must not silently rewrite past service events.

Show negative distance/time as Overdue rather than a confusing positive countdown.

3.3 Safety checklist, tyre pressure and chain slack

Treat the pre-ride checklist as a user-recorded inspection, not an automated guarantee that the motorcycle is safe.

Do not present 25–30 mm chain slack as universal; store the specification for the specific motorcycle and refer to its owner manual.

Store front and rear tyre pressure recommendations separately, including load conditions if relevant.

Label pressure guidance as cold-tyre pressure when that is what the manufacturer specifies.

Optionally record measured pressure, tread condition, inspection time, and notes.

If a safety-critical item fails, show it clearly and advise the rider not to ride until the issue is assessed or corrected.

3.4 Estimated fuel range

Use estimated usable fuel remaining multiplied by recent or selected mileage. Tank capacity alone is not enough. For example, 5 L of estimated usable fuel × 25 km/L = 125 km estimated range. Label this as an estimate and communicate uncertainty.

Track fuel consumed since the last full tank, or use a user-entered fuel-level estimate if supported.

Do not imply that the app has a direct fuel-level sensor unless one is actually connected.

Use a recent mileage window when enough valid data exists; otherwise show Not enough data.

Consider a conservative estimate and a confidence label instead of false precision.

3.5 Backup, restore, and document security

Include a backup schema version and app version in exported JSON.

Validate the complete backup before changing existing data.

Import transactionally: if validation or import fails, existing data must remain intact.

Prevent duplicate records when the same backup is imported twice; provide a clear replace/merge choice.

Include document images in the backup or clearly state that images are excluded.

Test large backups, corrupt JSON, missing fields, older backup versions, and interrupted imports.

Require confirmation before wipe/reset and clearly describe what will be deleted.

Consider app lock and encryption for personal documents, depending on the platform and threat model.

Compression reduces storage size but is not encryption or access control.

5. Dashboard and UX Recommendations

Keep the existing four metric tiles, but organize the dashboard around the rider's next decision.

Header: selected motorcycle, registration identifier, and motorcycle switcher.

Primary metrics: current odometer, recent mileage, estimated range, and next maintenance due.

Action-required strip: overdue maintenance, upcoming document expiry, and failed safety checks.

Quick actions: Log Refuel, Log Service, Pre-Ride Check, Add Expense, and Documents.

Monthly ownership cost: fuel plus maintenance/repair totals.

Show empty states with a clear next action rather than fabricated sample values.

Use distinct labels for measured, user-entered, and estimated data.

Keep important actions reachable on small screens and make touch targets accessible.

Data integrity rules

Use stable unique IDs and foreign keys/relationships to the motorcycle.

Recalculate derived metrics after changes to source records.

Use consistent date storage and test local time-zone transitions.

Use transactions for multi-record changes and restore operations.

Validate numeric fields, date ranges, units, and required fields.

Test storage quotas, database migrations, application restarts, offline operation, and failed writes.

10. Final Recommendation

Do not prioritize AI diagnosis or cloud synchronization before the basic records are reliable. First stabilize fuel calculations, maintenance scheduling, record editing/deletion, offline persistence, and backup recovery. Then add reminders, ownership cost analytics, and a repair journal. These improvements provide everyday value while reducing the risk of misleading maintenance or mileage information.
